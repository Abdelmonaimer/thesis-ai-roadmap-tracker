How to read every paper

Read one paper at a time. On a first pass, read the abstract, introduction, key diagrams and conclusion, and skip derivations you don't yet understand. Then write five short notes.

1ProblemWhat gap does it address?
2MethodWhat did they build or propose?
3DataWhich robot, sensors, dataset?
4ResultWhat was measured, under which conditions?
5LimitationWhat does it not show?
Pass 1 · abstract, intro, figures, conclusionFive-line note in ZoteroPass 2 · equations, once the course covers them

Keep these precise in your proposal

QLoRA

Reduces the memory needed to fine-tune a quantized base model. It does not turn a 7B model into one with fewer than 1.5B parameters. For that, use distillation, structured pruning or a smaller architecture. Quantization changes weight precision; real speed-ups depend on hardware and software support.

10 Hz = 100 ms

Ten decisions per second leaves about 100 ms for a full cycle. Measure VLA inference separately from sensor capture, messaging, safety checks and low-level motor control.

PIML and safety

A physics-informed loss encourages consistency with equations. It does not prove that robot actions are safe, and a low physics residual does not prove real-greenhouse accuracy.

Scope

One greenhouse task, one edge platform. Confirm with your supervisor which data, greenhouse, robot and edge board actually exist before committing to the full plan.
